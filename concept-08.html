01 / The person behind the work

A little about me.

"I don't know where every road leads, but that's half the fun of taking it."

I think that sentence describes me better than any job title ever could.

I'm a 2026 marketing graduate from Loyola University Maryland, but there's a lot more to my story than what I studied in college.

I've always been curious about people. What makes someone tick? Why do we think the way we do? What makes us connect with certain people, places, and ideas? I love a good conversation, especially one that leaves me thinking about something differently than I did before.

Some of my favorite experiences have come from stepping outside what's familiar. Joining a rugby team when I didn't even know how to play the sport, co-founding a men's mental health group, studying abroad in Copenhagen, and traveling to new places have all taught me something different about people, leadership, and myself.

I've learned that some of the most valuable lessons happen when you're not necessarily looking for them. Life has a funny way of taking you places you never expected. I don't know exactly where my path leads, but I'm learning to enjoy that uncertainty. After all, who really knows what tomorrow holds?

That same curiosity carries into my professional interests. I've worked in e-commerce, marketing, and growth research, and lately I've been exploring how AI can help turn interesting ideas into practical solutions. I enjoy figuring out how things work, asking questions, and seeing what I can create along the way.

I'm ambitious about my future, but I don't want to become so focused on what's next that I forget to enjoy what's happening right now. I'm still young, still figuring things out, and still making plenty of mistakes along the way. But that's how you learn, isn't it?

I think it's important to keep looking forward on the path ahead, but not so much that you forget to look down and appreciate where your feet are right now.

There are too many people to meet, places to explore, and things to learn to think I've already figured it all out. And honestly, I think that's the exciting part.
